# Watering in a dry summer

The valley's soil drains fast on the slopes and holds a puddle in the low beds, so
"water it a bit" isn't very useful advice here. This is what keepers actually do.

## Little and often is usually wrong

A light daily sprinkle wets the top of the soil and nothing else, and it trains roots
to stay near the surface where they dry out fastest. A deep soak twice a week, in the
morning, does far more for most of the catalogue's varieties.

## Priority when water is short

1. Anything newly transplanted in the last two weeks — its roots haven't reached
   deeper moisture yet.
2. Anything flowering or setting fruit — this is when a dry spell costs you the crop,
   not just growth.
3. Established leafy crops last; they can coast on a deeper root system.

## Mulch

A couple of centimetres of mulch — grass clippings, straw, whatever's to hand — over
damp soil keeps that moisture in far longer than watering again. Put it down after
watering, not instead of it.

Fernhollow keeps a rain gauge at the potting shed if you want to compare notes with
other members before deciding whether your own bed needs the hose.
