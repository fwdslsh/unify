# Saving your own seed

Returning seed is what keeps the library stocked without buying in, so it's worth
doing properly rather than quickly.

## Let it finish

Seed saved too early won't germinate. Let pods, seed heads, or fruit go well past the
point you'd eat them — dry and brittle for pods and seed heads, soft and almost
overripe for fruiting crops like squash and tomatoes.

## Dry it properly

Spread seed on paper (not plastic, which traps damp) somewhere airy and out of direct
sun for one to two weeks, stirring it now and then. It's dry enough when a seed
snaps rather than bends.

## Label it

A packet of unlabelled seed is close to useless to the next member. Write on the
packet: the variety name exactly as it appears in the catalogue, the year, and your
name in case a seed keeper has a question about it.

## Bring back a share, not everything

Keep enough for your own next sowing, and bring the rest to a seed keeper at the
potting shed. A modest, well-dried, well-labelled share is worth more to the library
than a large damp one.

Fernhollow keeps every returned batch until a seed keeper has checked it's dry and
correctly labelled, before it goes back into the catalogue for lending.
