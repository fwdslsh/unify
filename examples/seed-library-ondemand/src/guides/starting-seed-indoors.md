# Starting seed indoors

Most of the tender varieties in the catalogue — squash, tomatoes, anything marked for
a late spring sowing — do better started indoors than sown straight into cold valley
soil.

## What you need

- Small pots or modules, not the packet itself — seed sits too deep in a full tray.
- A bright windowsill or a cold frame; a warm, dark cupboard makes weak, leggy plants.
- Peat-free compost. Fernhollow doesn't stock compost, but any general-purpose bag works.

## The method

1. Sow at the depth given on the variety's catalogue page — usually about twice the
   seed's own width.
2. Water from below by standing the pot in a shallow tray, so you don't wash small seed
   out of place.
3. Once true leaves appear (the second set, not the first pair), move seedlings to a
   cooler spot for a week before planting out — this "hardening off" stops shock when
   they meet the outside air.

> A windowsill that gets direct afternoon sun can cook young seedlings through the
> glass. Turn the tray or move it back a little on hot days.

Fernhollow keeps its indoor-sowing guidance short on purpose: a seed keeper can talk
you through your particular windowsill if the general rule above doesn't quite fit.
