# Six weeks in a weir pool

We put a sensor where the manual says not to: two metres above the pool below a small
weir, where the surface is never flat and often white. The question was not whether it
would read well — it would not — but whether it would say so.

It did. Over six weeks the distance sat within 20&nbsp;mm of a staff gauge read twice a
week, which is worse than the ±3&nbsp;mm we quote and entirely usable for a level alarm.
The interesting number was `samples`, which averaged 31 out of 40 against 38 at our calm
reference site, and dropped into the low twenties for the four hours after each rainfall
peak.

That is the pattern worth generalising: **the sample count degrades before the distance
does**. By the time a turbulent site starts returning obviously wrong distances, the count
has been telling you for hours. If you deploy above broken water, alarm on the count.

Two practical notes. Spray on the transducer face cost nothing measurable — the face is
recessed and it dried between bursts. And the tilt reading drifted 0.4&nbsp;degrees over the
six weeks, which turned out to be the scaffold tube settling into wet ground, not the
bracket.
