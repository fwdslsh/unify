title: Sowing under cover
description: "Getting an earlier start with seed trays, cold frames, and a windowsill."

Fernhollow keeps several early varieties in the catalogue precisely because a
windowsill or a cold frame will get you weeks ahead of sowing straight into open
ground.

## What counts as "under cover"

It doesn't need to be a greenhouse. A seed tray on a bright windowsill, a cold
frame against a south wall, or even a clear plastic box with a few holes poked in
the top will all raise a seedling well before the soil outside has warmed up.

## Compost and containers

Use a peat-free seed compost rather than garden soil — it's finer, holds moisture
more evenly, and carries far fewer weed seeds and pests. Small modules or old
yoghurt pots with drainage holes both work; just don't let anything sit in a
saucer of standing water.

## Hardening off

The single most common way to lose a good batch of seedlings is to move them
straight from a warm windowsill into open ground. Give them a week to ten days
outside in their pots first — out during the day, back in if there's a frost
forecast — before you plant them out for good.

## When to start

As a rough guide, sow tender crops like tomatoes six to eight weeks before your
last expected frost, and hardier brassicas four to six weeks before you plan to
plant them out. Check the sowing window on each variety's own page in the
catalogue — it varies more than you'd expect between varieties of the same
vegetable.
