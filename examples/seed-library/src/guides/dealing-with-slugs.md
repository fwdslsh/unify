---
title: Dealing with slugs
description: "Volunteer notes on keeping slugs off young seedlings without wiping out everything else."
---

Fernhollow keeps losing the odd tray of seedlings to slugs every year, so this
guide is less theory and more what has actually worked for our volunteers.

## Seedlings are the vulnerable point

A slug that would barely dent a mature plant can strip a young seedling
overnight. The worst losses almost always happen in the first two weeks after
planting out, so that's where it's worth putting the effort.

## Give plants a head start

The single biggest difference our volunteers report is simply planting out
larger, sturdier seedlings rather than tiny ones — a plant that's already a
handful of true leaves across can shrug off damage that would finish a smaller
one. That's one more reason to sow under cover rather than direct into open
ground.

## Physical barriers over anything else

Copper tape around pots, a ring of sharp grit, or simply a cut-down plastic
bottle over a new seedling all buy time while the plant establishes. None of
these are permanent fixes, but they don't need renewing every time it rains, and
they don't harm anything else in the plot.

## Go out after dark

Slugs feed mostly at night. Twenty minutes out with a torch after watering, a
couple of evenings in a row, will remove far more of them than any amount of
daytime patrolling — and you'll get a much better sense of how bad the problem
actually is before deciding whether to act further.
