---
title: "Early summer report: Bean production"
---

**June 15, 2026**

Fernhollow keeps careful records of when plants begin flowering. This month marks the transition from planting to production.

## Bean situation

All four Fabaceae varieties are flowering:

- **Carlin Pea:** Heavy flower set, first tiny pods visible
- **Champion of England:** Climbing beautifully, reaching the top of supports
- **Robinson Broad Bean:** Flowers opening, bees visiting regularly
- **Cherokee Wax and Lazy Housewife:** Just starting to flower

First picks will happen in 2-3 weeks.

## Tomatoes in development

Seedlings from May and June sowings are growing vigorously in the nursery:

- **Latah, Stupice, Black Krim:** Ready for transplanting this week
- **Aunt Ruby, Pink Brandywine:** One week behind, transplanting next week
- **Bere Barley:** Still indoors, growing strongly

First flowers expected in 3-4 weeks as transplanted plants establish.

## Squash ready to plant

Uchiki Kuri, Crown Prince, Boston Marrow, and Table Dainty seedlings are hardened off and ready for final positions. First flowers expected in 6-7 weeks.

## Kale and brassicas

Early sowings (April-May) are developing heads:
- **Bere Kale, Daubenton:** Good head formation
- **Nine Star Broccoli:** Producing small side shoots, main head developing
- **Purple Sprouting, Red Russian, Ragged Jack:** Transplant-sized

## Alliums

Onion sets from March sowings are bulking. Welsh Onion is producing abundant foliage for eating.

## Carrot news

Direct sowings from April are thinning easily for salads. Chantenay Red is particularly fine-textured—excellent eating variety.

## What's happening

We're at that peak energy moment when everything is growing and flowering and the garden feels alive. Pests are manageable, disease is minimal, and the bees are busy.

## Next focus

Succession planting continues. Members are sowing late brassicas for autumn harvest.
