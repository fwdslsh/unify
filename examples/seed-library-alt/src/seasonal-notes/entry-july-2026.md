title: "July notes: Tomatoes flowering"
---

**July 28, 2026**

Fernhollow keeps track of every flowering moment. The tomatoes have reached a crucial stage—what flowers now will become the harvest we save seeds from.

## The stage we're in

**Indeterminate varieties** (Aunt Ruby, Pink Brandywine, Black Krim, Latah): These continue flowering in clusters up the stem. Some first flowers are setting fruit.

**Determinate and short-season varieties** (Stupice, Yellow Pear, Bere Barley): These are already heavy with small green fruit.

**Kale and brassicas:** Late June sowings are at the 4-6 leaf stage, looking strong. P. Aluko reports her germination at 95% on Red Russian.

## Pollination watch

The warm weather has brought plenty of bumblebees. We're observing good natural pollination in outdoor plants. Greenhouse plants need hand-pollination—J. Vance reports shaking her flowers daily at midday for best results.

## Water requirements

The dry spell continues. Daily watering is necessary. We're recommending members mulch around plants to conserve moisture.

## Pest report

Minor aphid pressure on some beans. No significant issues otherwise. The warm weather has reduced fungal problems.

## What's ready to harvest

Chantenay Red carrots are beginning to size up nicely. Several members report being able to thin roots for salads.

Early batches of Carlin and Champion of England peas are over, but members are enjoying the harvest. One keeper will save seed from late-flowering plants.

## Planning ahead

Members who borrowed summer varieties should be thinking about autumn. If you want to borrow cold-season seeds, let us know by August 5 so we can reserve stock.

## Next update

August brings peak harvest season. We'll report on seed maturity and first returns.
