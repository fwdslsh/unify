---
title: Winter Gardening in the Valley
---

Fernhollow keeps many varieties that thrive in our cool valley winters. While frost and shorter days challenge summer crops, hardy vegetables actually improve in flavor after a frost.

## Valley climate advantages

Our south-facing slopes and shelter from the north mean winter can be surprisingly productive if you choose the right varieties.

## Best winter vegetables

### Brassicas
Kales, broccoli, and other brassicas are superstars in winter:
- **Bere Kale** and **Ragged Jack**: Sweeter after a hard frost
- **Purple Sprouting**: Produces over many weeks starting in spring
- **Red Russian Kale**: Hardy and prolific

### Alliums
Onions and Welsh Onions can be overwintered:
- Sow in August for winter/spring harvest
- Welsh Onion produces foliage year-round

### Root crops
Carrots and other roots stored in the ground are sweeter in winter:
- **Autumn King Carrot**: Stores well underground through freezing

## Winter growing timeline

**August:** Sow autumn brassicas and onions
**September:** Continue sowing brassicas, prepare beds
**October–November:** Final sowings of fast varieties
**December–February:** Harvest from established plants
**March:** Spring brassicas like Purple Sprouting start producing

## Protecting plants

- Use cloches or fleece over young plants to speed growth
- Mulch around plants to insulate soil
- Water in the morning to avoid frost damage
- Harvest regularly to encourage new growth
