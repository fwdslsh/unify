---
title: Saving Seeds for Next Year
---

Fernhollow keeps its heritage alive through seed saving. When you save seeds from our varieties and return them, you're part of a continuous chain stretching back decades.

## Why save seeds?

- Seeds from plants adapted to your garden will thrive next year
- Heritage varieties aren't available in garden centres
- It closes the loop—you borrow, grow, return, and receive again
- Seed saving connects you to deep gardening tradition

## The seed saving year

### Summer: Select and mark
As your plants grow, mark the healthiest, most vigorous plants with string or labels. These are the ones to save from.

### Late summer/autumn: Let them mature
Stop harvesting from marked plants. Let the seeds develop fully—they should be hard and brown, not soft or green.

### Harvest time
- **Dry seeds** (beans, peas, kale): Let pods dry on the plant, then collect when crispy
- **Wet seeds** (tomatoes, cucumber): Ferment in water for a few days, then rinse and dry thoroughly
- **Roots and bulbs** (onions, carrots): These need to flower in their second year

### Drying
Spread seeds on paper plates in a warm, dry place for 2-3 weeks. They're ready when they snap, not bend.

### Storage
Store in paper packets in a cool, dry place over winter. Label with:
- Variety name
- Date harvested
- Your name (if you're the keeper)

## Varieties that cross-pollinate

Some vegetables cross-pollinate and need isolation distance if growing multiple varieties:

- **Brassicas** (kale, broccoli): 100m isolation
- **Alliums** (onions): 100m isolation
- **Beans and peas**: Usually self-pollinating, safe to grow together

Ask us about isolation distances for specific varieties.

## Return your seeds

Bring your dried seeds back to us in labeled paper packets. We'll record them and make them available to other members next season.
