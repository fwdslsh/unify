Fernhollow keeps this guide to help gardeners unlock the potential of seeds from our collection.

## Why start from seed?

Starting your vegetables from seed is more economical than buying seedlings, gives you access to rare heritage varieties like ours, and connects you directly to the growing process. Whether you're growing in a garden, raised bed, or even a sunny windowsill, these principles apply.

## Timing matters

Different vegetables need different sowing times:

- **Spring vegetables** (peas, broad beans, onions): Sow in February through April
- **Summer vegetables** (tomatoes, beans, squash): Sow in April through June
- **Autumn crops** (kale, cabbage): Sow in June through August

Check the variety information in our catalogue for specific dates for each type.

## Basic steps

### 1. Prepare your soil
Use good quality seed compost, not garden soil. Fill your pots or trays and water gently.

### 2. Sow your seeds
Read the packet! Some seeds need light to germinate, others prefer darkness. Plant at the depth shown.

### 3. Keep conditions right
Most seeds need warmth and moisture to germinate. A heated propagator helps, but a windowsill works too.

### 4. Provide light
Once they sprout, give seedlings as much light as possible to prevent them getting leggy and weak.

### 5. Harden off
Before moving seedlings outside, gradually expose them to outdoor conditions over 10 days.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Planting too deep:** Most seeds should be covered with just their own depth of soil
- **Overwatering:** Soggy compost causes rot. Water from below if possible
- **Too little light:** Pale, stretched seedlings won't produce well
- **Transplanting too late:** Don't let seedlings become root-bound
